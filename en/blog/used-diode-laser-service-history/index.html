Buying equipment

Used diode lasers – why buy equipment with a service history?

Buying a used laser can be an excellent option. Professional equipment may cost substantially less than new and remain useful for years. There is one condition: knowing exactly what you are buying.

9 September 2026 • approximately 8 min read

A used laser need not be a poor laser

I often hear that used aesthetic equipment means problems. I disagree.

A good diode laser can operate for many years. The same applies to other professional aesthetic equipment.

What matters most is its condition, how it was used and whether it was regularly serviced.

When buying used equipment, I would therefore look beyond manufacturing year, housing appearance and screen figures.

A more important question is: what has happened to this device over the past few years?

The biggest problem when buying used equipment is missing information

Imagine a familiar situation.

You find a used diode laser online at an attractive price. It looks excellent in photographs. The seller says everything works, it saw little use and never broke down.

The problem is that the buyer usually cannot readily verify this.

We do not know whether the handpiece was repaired, the diodes replaced or cooling, power supply or electronics problems occurred.

Nor do we know who carried out previous repairs or whether they were done correctly.

For me, service history is therefore one of the most important factors when buying used professional equipment.

Service history reveals more than appearance

A housing can be cleaned thoroughly, a screen polished and a device prepared beautifully for photographs.

But a photograph does not reveal what happened inside.

With a service history, we can check replaced components, previous faults, repairs and the last service date.

This makes a major difference: the buyer is no longer purchasing entirely in the dark.

Documentation really matters for professional equipment

A diode laser or other aesthetic device is not an ordinary household appliance.

It is professional equipment used with clients, often every day.

It is worth knowing who serviced and repaired it and whether they have appropriate qualifications and records of the work.

Laser Tech Service holds the certificates and qualifications required for the laser equipment servicing work we perform.

For the client, this means the device is more than an item being sold. Its condition is checked by people who can continue servicing it afterwards.

Price matters, but should not be the only criterion

I understand that price is important when buying equipment.

Especially when opening a clinic, introducing a service or expanding a business.

The cheapest laser, however, is not always the least expensive solution.

If the handpiece, cooling or electronics need repair a few weeks later, the initial saving can quickly disappear.

Equipment should therefore be bought with a clear understanding.

Know what you are buying, its history and who serviced it. Above all, know whom to call if a technical problem occurs a year or two later.

Buying equipment? Ask about its history first

If I could give one piece of advice to someone seeking a used laser, it would be not to begin with “how much does it cost?”

First ask what was repaired, who serviced it, whether service records exist and who will support it after purchase.

With specific answers, a used laser can be a very good purchase.

If nobody knows its past, a low price should prompt closer inspection rather than be the sole reason to buy.
