The Primelase HR Excellence by Cocoon Medical is a professional laser platform used for applications including unwanted hair reduction. Depending on the applicator configuration, peak optical power can reach up to 4,800 W.

High-power pulsed operation requires effective thermal management. Primelase incorporates a multi-circuit cooling architecture involving thermoelectric modules, heat exchangers, circulation pumps and a radiator fan.

## Primelase cooling system architecture

Technical documentation for the Primelase platform describes three coolant circuits:

- A laser diode cooling circuit.

- An applicator contact-tip temperature control circuit.

- A heat rejection circuit serving the hot side of the thermoelectric modules.

The first two circuits use thermoelectric cooling based on the Peltier effect. The third transports thermal energy to an air-cooled radiator, where it is transferred to the surrounding environment.

This architecture enables independent temperature regulation of different device components.

## Peltier modules and thermal balance

A thermoelectric module transfers heat from its cold side to its hot side. The electrical energy consumed by the module adds to the thermal load that must be rejected.

The energy balance can be represented as:

Qhot = Qcold + Pelectric

The radiator must therefore dissipate both the heat extracted from the cooled components and the additional energy associated with thermoelectric operation.

Increasing hot-side temperatures can reduce a thermoelectric module's ability to maintain the required cold-side temperature. Consequently, effective final heat rejection is important for the thermal stability of the entire laser platform.

## The function of the main radiator fan

![Radiator cooling fan](https://lasertechservice.pl/img/realizacje/primelase-hr-excellence/primelase-radiator-cooling-fan.webp)

The axial fan forces air through the radiator's heat exchange surface.

Coolant transports thermal energy to the heat exchanger, while airflow enables heat transfer to the surrounding environment through forced convection.

The efficiency of this process depends on airflow rate, coolant and ambient temperatures, radiator cleanliness, bearing and impeller condition, and the aerodynamic resistance of the ventilation path.

Unlike smaller electronic compartment fans, the radiator fan supports the final heat rejection stage of the liquid cooling system.

## Cooling fan bearing wear

Fan bearings support the rotating assembly and reduce rotational resistance.

Progressive bearing wear may cause excessive mechanical clearance, vibration, unusual noise and irregular impeller rotation. Advanced deterioration may result in reduced rotational speed or complete fan seizure.

Typical symptoms include:

- Unusual mechanical noise.

- Rattling, knocking or grinding sounds.

- Increased vibration of the radiator assembly.

- Reduced airflow.

- Deteriorating thermal stability.

Noise alone does not conclusively identify bearing failure. Impeller imbalance, loose mountings and mechanical contact with the housing may produce similar symptoms.

## Radiator contamination

![Fan and contaminated radiator](https://lasertechservice.pl/img/realizacje/primelase-hr-excellence/primelase-radiator-dust-contamination.webp)

Dust, textile fibres and other deposits on radiator fins restrict airflow and impair heat exchange.

This condition may occur even when circulation pumps and the fan remain operational.

When radiator contamination is combined with mechanical fan deterioration, the overall heat rejection capacity may decline further.

Potential consequences include longer temperature stabilisation times, restricted operating parameters, thermal warnings and protective shutdowns.

Prolonged operation under unsuitable thermal conditions may accelerate component degradation.

## Technical diagnostics and maintenance

Professional cooling system diagnostics should include assessment of the mechanical condition of the fan, radiator airflow restrictions, hydraulic circuits and temperature stability.

Following repairs, functional verification should be performed according to the requirements applicable to the specific device revision.

Preventive maintenance helps identify mechanical deterioration before it develops into more serious cooling system problems.

If abnormal mechanical noise or reduced cooling performance occurs, the equipment should be taken out of operation until it has been assessed. Internal repairs should be performed by appropriately qualified personnel.

## Frequently asked questions

### Does unusual Primelase fan noise indicate a malfunction?

It may indicate bearing deterioration or another mechanical problem. The exact cause must be established through diagnostics.

### Is a functioning circulation pump sufficient for effective cooling?

No. The pump circulates coolant, but the collected heat must subsequently be transferred to the surroundings through the heat exchanger.

### Can a contaminated radiator cause laser overheating?

Yes. Contamination restricts airflow and reduces heat exchange efficiency.

### Can the fan be repaired without replacing the entire assembly?

Component-level repair may be possible depending on the fan design, motor condition, impeller condition and availability of suitable replacement parts.
