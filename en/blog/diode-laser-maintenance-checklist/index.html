Diode Laser Maintenance Checklist: Cooling System Inspection

2026-11-21

Why every clinic needs a maintenance checklist

Diode laser equipment may operate for many hours each day.

Regular maintenance is essential for identifying developing problems before they result in unexpected downtime.

The cooling system deserves particular attention because it helps maintain suitable operating temperatures for sensitive components.

A checklist also helps distinguish routine operator inspections from procedures requiring qualified service technicians.

This guide is general and does not replace the manufacturer's service documentation.

1. Before starting the device

Operators should inspect the equipment for visible damage and abnormal conditions.

Check the display for warnings, verify temperature readings and look for visible coolant leaks.

Where required by the manufacturer, check the coolant level.

Do not continue treatment if the equipment indicates a cooling fault.

2. Temperature monitoring

The correct cooling temperature depends on the laser model.

There is no universal 22°C operating requirement for all diode lasers.

Displayed values must be interpreted using the manufacturer's specifications.

3. Coolant circulation

Some devices display flow measurements or sensor values.

These may be expressed in different units or represented by proprietary indicators.

A low-flow warning requires investigation.

Never bypass a cooling-related safety interlock.

4. Cooling filter inspection

Inspect accessible filters for discoloration, deposits, leaks and damaged connections.

Visible contamination may indicate the need for maintenance.

However, filter color alone cannot determine the chemical composition of deposits or accurately quantify flow restriction.

Follow the manufacturer's replacement procedure.

5. Coolant quality

The cooling medium must meet the device's specifications.

Where purified water is required, conductivity measurements may help assess water quality.

Acceptable conductivity limits vary by equipment and should be verified against the manufacturer's documentation.

6. Pump and tubing inspection

A qualified technician should inspect the pump, tubing, fittings and hydraulic connections.

Look for leakage, unusual noises, trapped air and irregular circulation.

Cooling faults can originate from several components, not only the filter.

7. Filter and coolant replacement

Before opening the system, the equipment must be safely shut down and disconnected from its power supply.

Use compatible replacement parts and approved sealing materials.

Where a filter has a marked flow direction, install it accordingly.

The filling and air-removal procedure must follow the device's service manual.

8. Post-maintenance testing

After servicing, verify leak-tightness, coolant circulation, sensor readings and safety functions.

Laser emission testing must only be performed by qualified personnel using the appropriate safety procedures.

9. Maintenance records

Document the service date, equipment identification, completed work and replaced components.

Record relevant readings, including pulse counter values where available.

Accurate maintenance records support future troubleshooting and service planning.

Suggested inspection schedule

Daily: check warnings, temperature readings and visible faults.

Periodically: inspect accessible filters, ventilation openings and tubing as instructed by the manufacturer.

During professional servicing: inspect the pump, coolant quality, connections and cooling performance.

At manufacturer-defined intervals: replace filters, coolant and other consumables.

Conclusion

A structured maintenance checklist helps clinics maintain their diode laser equipment responsibly.

Consistent inspections and timely professional servicing can reduce the risk of unexpected failures.
